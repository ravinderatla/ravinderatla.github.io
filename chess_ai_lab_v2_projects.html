Chess AI Lab — Research Program

A longitudinal behavioral research program using chess as a model system · C1 → C10 · R1 → R3
Central research question
How does human chess decision behavior vary between individuals and skill levels, how does it change over time and across performance states, and can these behavioral representations explain or improve individual learning?
Applied question → Can those behavioral representations be used to produce more effective personalized learning interventions than generic engine-based recommendations?

What changed from v1

From a list of tools → one research program

The projects are no longer treated as independent tools. They form a single longitudinal research program with a central scientific question. C1 and R1 are the critical scientific core. Everything else builds on or applies their output.

C10 is new — the missing subjective layer

Historical PGN cannot tell you whether you were tired, stressed, or distracted. C10 adds a 30–60 second pre-game self-annotation protocol that finally links your objective behavioral data to your subjective state. This is what makes the C9 performance-state model genuinely interpretable.

Psychological labels removed

C8 no longer uses labels like "confidence", "intuition", "panic", or "deep calculation" — these cannot be inferred from PGN alone. Instead it uses observable temporal states: very fast, normal, deliberative, low-clock, post-error, recovery. C9 similarly models "performance residual" rather than claiming to detect tilt or mood.

C3, C4 deferred · R3 merged into C8

C3 (repertoire builder) and C4 (opponent profiling) are now treated as applications of the research, not primary research programs. R3 (behavioral dataset) is no longer standalone — it is the dataset output of C8, released conditionally if the labeling methodology becomes defensible.

C1 Behavioral fingerprint + drift tracker
CriticalFoundation
Determine how stable and individually distinctive human chess behavior is, and detect meaningful behavioral changes over time.
Abstract, research questions & concepts▼
Updated abstract
Individual chess players may exhibit measurable behavioral signatures across their games — move timing, position preferences, decision patterns, and move-selection tendencies. This project constructs a longitudinal behavioral representation from the personal game history and tests which parts are genuinely player-specific versus explained by rating, opening, time control, opponent, position, and game phase. The original hypothesis that behavioral change necessarily precedes rating improvement is treated as a hypothesis to test, not an assumption.
Key methodological principle: Behavior = Player + Rating + Opening + TimeControl + Position + Opponent + Noise. The fingerprint must be validated as a genuine representation before being used as a predictor.
Research questions
  • How stable are individual behavioral fingerprints over 6–12 months after controlling for game context?
  • Which behavioral dimensions are genuinely player-specific, and which are primarily explained by rating, opening, time control, opponent, position, or phase?
  • Which behavioral changes accompany periods of improvement, stagnation, or decline?
  • Do meaningful behavioral changes occur before measurable rating changes?
  • Can interpretable behavioral features capture the same player-specific signal as learned style embeddings (Elo-disentangled, Maia4All)?

C8 Temporal decision behavior
HighReframed
Model how think time, clock state, and position difficulty interact to produce good and bad decisions — without psychological labels.
Abstract, research questions & concepts▼
Updated abstract
Chess timestamps provide unusually detailed behavioral information, but think time is an observable measure of decision duration — not a direct measurement of psychological state. This project studies how normalized think time interacts with position complexity, game phase, clock pressure, and move quality, identifying personal decision-time curves and observable temporal states without claiming to reveal confidence, panic, intuition, or other latent states.
Terminology change: Use observable states — very fast · normal · deliberative · extreme deliberation · low-clock · severe time pressure · post-error · recovery-after-error — not psychological labels like confidence, intuition, or panic.
Research questions
  • What is the relationship between normalized think time and move quality conditional on position complexity?
  • How does this relationship differ between tactical, positional, theoretical, and endgame positions?
  • What is each player's personal time-pressure threshold?
  • Is the relationship nonlinear — with both too little and excessive deliberation associated with poorer outcomes in different contexts?
  • How does a player's time-allocation function change across rating and performance states?
  • Can observable decision contexts be reliably distinguished without assigning unsupported psychological labels?

C9 Longitudinal performance-state modeling
HighPromoted
Model short-term performance states across games — persistence, recovery, and performance cascades — using performance residuals, not psychological constructs.
Abstract, research questions & concepts▼
Updated abstract
The personal game history contains periods of unusually strong performance followed by sharp declines. C9 investigates this without assuming the cause is tilt, fatigue, mood, or any psychological construct that PGN alone cannot identify. The central concept is a performance-state phenotype: define an expected performance baseline accounting for opponent strength, position difficulty, time control, opening, phase, and context — then study the residual. Performance residual = observed − expected. This makes it possible to identify degraded or elevated states without claiming to know their cause.
Key distinction: Do not attempt to distinguish cognitive fatigue, emotional tilt, and genuine difficulty from PGN alone. That distinction becomes possible only when additional state information is collected — which motivates C10.
Research questions
  • Does game N predict game N+1 performance beyond opponent strength and game context?
  • How long do performance states persist across subsequent games?
  • Are there identifiable performance cascades across consecutive games?
  • Does rest duration predict performance recovery?
  • Can early-game behavior predict whether final performance will fall below the player's expected baseline?
  • Can degraded-performance signatures be identified without making causal psychological claims?

C2 Mistake-pattern coach with human context
HighSimplified
Predict recurring personal mistakes and test whether examples from stronger humans provide useful learning information beyond engine-only feedback.
Abstract, research questions & concepts▼
Updated abstract
The project builds a personalized mistake model from the player's game history and uses it to retrieve human examples from stronger players in comparable situations. The central research question is not whether an elaborate RL system can select puzzles — but whether personalized human examples actually improve transfer and learning. The PivotRL-heavy implementation is deferred until the intervention itself has evidence behind it.
Recommended order: Personal mistake model → human-example retrieval → controlled learning intervention → measure transfer → only then consider RL/adaptive optimization.
Research questions
  • Can a personal mistake model predict future errors better than generic centipawn-loss thresholds?
  • Do players 200–400 Elo stronger exhibit systematically different decision behavior in positions where the learner frequently errs?
  • Do stronger-human examples improve transfer to unseen positions compared with engine-only feedback?
  • Does adaptive selection based on personal weaknesses outperform random or generic puzzle selection?
  • How much of the benefit comes from retrieval of human examples versus the explanation attached to them?

R1 Rating-band behavioral atlas
CriticalMain paper
Create a population-level map of human decision behavior across skill levels while controlling for game context.
Abstract, research questions & concepts▼
Updated abstract
Rather than claiming to directly provide an "improvement pathway," R1 establishes which behavioral dimensions are associated with chess skill and which behavioral changes are associated with subsequent rating progression. The contribution is strongest as a population-level, interpretable, longitudinal behavioral analysis of chess players across skill levels — using the full Lichess DB segmented into 100-Elo bands.
Framing change: Do not make "the missing piece from Maia-2" the novelty claim. The safer, stronger contribution is: a population-level, interpretable, longitudinal analysis of human chess behavior and skill transitions.
Research questions
  • Which behavioral dimensions are most strongly associated with skill after controlling for game context?
  • Are behavioral transitions across skill levels gradual, nonlinear, or concentrated around particular thresholds?
  • Which behavioral features predict subsequent rating progression after conditioning on current rating and game context?
  • Do future improvers show measurable behavioral changes before their rating increases?
  • Which players systematically overperform or underperform relative to what their rating would predict?

C10 Prospective state-of-play N-of-1 study
NEWHigh priority
Determine how much within-player variation in chess performance can be explained by pre-game state and situational context — by adding the subjective layer that PGN alone cannot provide.

Why this project exists

The ~12K-game historical dataset provides an unusually strong objective behavioral baseline, but historical PGN does not tell us whether the player was tired, stressed, irritated, distracted, well-rested, or under external pressure before a game. C10 adds exactly that missing layer through a prospective self-recording protocol.

Core research question

To what extent can within-player variation in chess performance be explained by pre-game state, situational factors, and temporal decision behavior?

Secondary research questions
  • Does sleep quality predict performance after controlling for opponent, position, and time control?
  • Does work or household pressure affect performance independently of clock pressure?
  • Can poor-state games be distinguished from games where performance deteriorates primarily because of time pressure?
  • Does pre-game state predict the player's think-time/accuracy curve?
  • Are there recurring personal performance-state phenotypes?
  • How much apparent short-term rating fluctuation can be explained by state rather than skill?
  • Are these relationships stable over several months?

The three performance failure modes C10 can distinguish

A — Time-pressure failure

Good pre-game state → normal performance → errors increase only when clock becomes low

B — Poor-state performance

Poor sleep / stress / low readiness → performance already below baseline even with adequate time

C — State-dependent impairment

Player knows the ideas, has sufficient time, but familiar positions are played materially below their own baseline

The third category should be described behaviorally rather than diagnosed psychologically.
Core methodological principles

1 — Control context before interpreting behavior

At minimum, every behavioral measure must account for: player rating, opponent rating, opening / ECO, time control, game phase, position complexity, color, move number, and session position. Behavior that looks like a personal trait may simply reflect contextual variation.

2 — Separate observable behavior from latent psychology

PGN CAN show
  • Think time
  • Clock state
  • Move choice
  • Evaluation change
  • Sequence of errors
  • Recovery patterns
PGN CANNOT establish
  • Confidence
  • Anxiety / panic
  • Deep calculation
  • Intuition
  • Fatigue or mood
  • Motivation

C10 provides a path toward studying those relationships by adding prospective self-report.

3 — Treat improvement prediction as an empirical question

Do not assume behavioral shift → rating improvement. Test whether it holds after controlling for current rating and game context. The relationship is a hypothesis, not a premise.

4 — Prefer interpretable models first

Establish the signal with mixed-effects models, regression, survival/transition analysis, calibrated classifiers, effect sizes, and interpretable feature importance. More complex neural models follow once the underlying relationships are established.

5 — Distinguish prediction from causation

A behavioral feature predicting future rating does not mean changing that feature will cause rating improvement. This distinction is especially important for C1 and R1 when framing research contributions.

C3 Personalized repertoire builder
Deferred — application
Use empirically measured behavioral strengths and weaknesses to personalize opening recommendations.
Why deferred▼
C3 is primarily an application of C1 and R1, not a standalone research program. Opening outcomes are heavily confounded by opponent strength, preparation, familiarity, frequency, color, time control, and game context. Research value is limited unless these factors are controlled carefully. Build this after C1/R1 establish which behavioral dimensions are meaningful.
Single research question: Does conditioning opening recommendations on a player's empirically measured behavioral strengths improve practical outcomes compared with theory-first recommendations?
